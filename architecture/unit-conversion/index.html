    Unit Conversion¶

    Product.stock_pieces is the single source of truth for stock, and it is always counted in individual pieces — regardless of whether the product is sold by the piece, by the bundle, or by weight. Product.pieces_per_unit is the conversion factor between "one selling unit" and "pieces deducted." Every stock change (quick-sell, itemized sale, restock) computes pieces = quantity × pieces_per_unit and passes that through services.inventory.apply_stock_change — nothing writes stock_pieces any other way.

    flowchart LR
    A["Product defined<br/>unit_type + pieces_per_unit"] --> B["Sale input<br/>(bundle, piece, or measure quantity)"]
    B --> C["Conversion<br/>pieces = quantity × pieces_per_unit"]
    C --> D["Single stock count<br/>stock_pieces −= pieces"]
    D --> E["Stock re-displayed<br/>in the product's own unit_type"]

    Worked example — bundle¶

    A seller lists pandesal as a bundle product with pieces_per_unit = 25 (one bundle = 25 pieces of bread) and stock_pieces = 250 (10 bundles on hand).

    Step Value
    Buyer/seller sells 2 bundles
    Pieces deducted 2 × 25 = 50
    New stock_pieces 250 − 50 = 200
    Displayed remaining stock 200 / 25 = 8 bundles

    ProductForm (frontend/src/features/inventory/ProductForm.jsx) labels this field "Pieces per Bundle" and only shows it for bundle products — piece and measure products always send pieces_per_unit = 1 and the field is hidden.

    Selling a bundle per piece¶

    A sale line for a bundle product can set byPiece: true (SaleItemCreate.by_piece, and BuyRequest.by_piece for buyer orders). services.inventory.build_sale_item then snapshots it as a piece line: unit_type_snapshot = piece, list price and cost = per_piece_cents(price, pieces_per_unit) (integer half-up, so ₱100 / 3 = 3333 cents), and pieces_deducted = quantity. Receipts, void/refund/cancel and the forecast read the snapshot and pieces_deducted, so they need no special case. by_piece on a non-bundle product raises InvalidSaleUnitError (400). create_sale sums pieces_deducted per product across lines before deducting, so a bundle line and a piece line of the same product are validated together.

    On the frontend, saleLineUnit(product, byPiece) in features/sales/salesFormat.js gives a line's price, cost and unit, and the New Sale cart keys lines by product and mode.

    Analytics "units sold" still sums sale_items.quantity, so a piece line counts like a bundle there.

    Measured products¶

    For unit_type = "measure" (meat by the kg, fabric by the yard, rope by the meter, …) the seller picks the unit, stored as Product.unit_label (≤ 16 chars, required for measure products). To allow fractional amounts without floats, everything quantity-shaped is stored in thousandths of that unit (MEASURE_QUANTITY_SCALE = 1000 in services/inventory.py):

    Field Meaning for a measure product
    stock_pieces thousandths of the unit (50500 = 50.5 yd)
    low_stock_threshold thousandths of the unit
    sale / restock quantity thousandths of the unit (1500 = 1.5 yd)
    pieces_per_unit always 1 (enforced: 400 otherwise), so stock deducted = quantity
    price_cents per whole unit (12000 = ₱120/yd)

    Line totals use line_total_cents(price_cents, quantity, unit_type) in services/inventory.py, which divides by the scale with integer half-up rounding (1500 × 12000 / 1000 = 18000). The frontend mirrors it as lineTotalCents in frontend/src/lib/format.js, alongside formatQuantity / parseQuantityInput / sanitizeQuantityInput for converting between the stored integer and the decimal text sellers type. sale_items.unit_label_snapshot keeps receipts stable if a product's unit label is edited later.

    The migration c3e1a7d9f2b4 converted the old grams ("Per Kilo") type: grams were already thousandths of a kg, so stock and quantities were kept as-is, unit_label set to kg, and per-gram prices multiplied by 1000 to per-kg prices (line totals unchanged).

    Why this matters¶

    Because stock_pieces is always pieces, low-stock comparisons (stock_pieces <= low_stock_threshold), the demand forecast, and analytics all operate on one consistent unit regardless of how a product is sold — there is no unit-mismatch bug possible between "10 bundles low-stock" and "250 pieces low-stock" because the threshold is also stored in pieces.
